Perception+sense
Perception meets sense.

A course for building real intuition.

The idea

Facts are easy. Sense is hard.

You know a marathon is long and Picasso was important. But how long? Important compared to whom? Perceptense covers the things you should have a feel for: weight and distance, but also art, history, technology, wellness, and the everyday systems around you. Anchors are real-world reference points your brain can latch onto. Once you know a few solid ones, every other number snaps into place by comparison.

1 second A heartbeat. Roughly one full breath in or out.
1 minute Boiling an egg for a soft yolk. A single song chorus.
1 hour A lecture. A short film. About 3,600 heartbeats.
1 day Earth turns once. You sleep for ≈ a third of it.

Calculators let you plug in your own numbers. The point isn't the math — it's seeing how the answer changes when you tweak the inputs.

Visual scale turns abstract numbers into things you can see. Bars, maps, and side-by-side layouts make orders of magnitude obvious instead of theoretical.

Heart rate vs. body size. The smaller the animal, the harder its heart works.

Q&A reveals are quick estimation prompts. Form a guess in your head, then tap to see what's actually true. The gap between guess and truth is where intuition gets built.

If you stacked every person alive today head-to-toe, how tall would the stack be?

About 13.7 million km — tall enough to wrap around Earth's equator 340 times, or reach the Moon 35 times over.

How many seconds are in a human lifetime (80 years)?

About 2.5 billion seconds. We typically think in years, but per-second it's a strikingly finite number.

Interactive games turn classic puzzles into something you can actually play. The Monty Hall problem only really clicks once you've felt the math by tapping a few times — switching wins ~2/3 of the time.

Animations show what static text can't. A wave isn't a number — it's something that moves. Modules use small live visuals to make ideas like interference, orbits, and feedback loops feel concrete.

Two-source wave interference, simplified. Where the rings meet in step, the wave reinforces; where they meet out of step, they cancel — the heart of the double-slit experiment in Module 43.

I built Perceptense because I kept catching myself nodding along at numbers I didn't really understand, gigatons of CO2, light-years, basis points, GDP. The internet is great at facts and bad at feel. This is my attempt at a small antidote: short, playful modules you can poke at, instead of long articles you skim.

Everything here is hand-built, single-file HTML, and free forever. If a module clicks for you, or breaks, I'd love to hear about it.
